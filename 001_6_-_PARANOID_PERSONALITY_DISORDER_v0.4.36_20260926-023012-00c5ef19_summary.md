## Key Distinctions

- Paranoid personality disorder vs. paranoid schizophrenia: The chapter distinguishes them by noting that paranoid schizophrenia involves a "serious break with reality," bizarre delusions, and auditory hallucinations, whereas paranoid personality disorder is characterized by unwarranted suspiciousness without these psychotic features (pp. 155–156). (p. ~137)
- Paranoid personality disorder vs. phobic character: The distinction lies in the object of avoidance and the nature of the threat; the phobic character avoids situations generally with "fearfulness but lacks the personalization and intentionality (It is a danger, but I do not know why") seen in paranoid personalities, who specifically accuse others of malicious intent (pp. 156–157). (p. ~141)
- Paranoid personality disorder vs. compulsive personality: Both share rigidity and restricted spontaneity, but the compulsive character views demands as internal moral mandates ("pressure to conform emanates from within"), whereas the paranoid individual views such demands as unjustified external impositions (pp. 157–158). (p. ~146)
- Paranoid personality disorder vs. narcissistic personality: While both share ambition and "narcissistic rage, the chapter distinguishes them by noting that the narcissist displays a charming social veneer and seductiveness, whereas the paranoid is characterized by angry negativism, haughty aloofness, or brooding withdrawal" and pervasive mistrust (pp. 158–159). (p. ~150)
- Paranoid personality disorder vs. antisocial personality: The chapter highlights that while there is overlap in moralistic attitudes, the paranoid individual lacks the "chronicity of antisocial behavior—and the child to adult continuum—found in the psychopath," with their acts being more consistent with projection than the "stimulating quality" of antisocial personality (p. 159).
