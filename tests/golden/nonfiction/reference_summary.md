Effective observation is the cornerstone of intelligence work: noticing details that others miss distinguishes the professional operative from the amateur. Casing is the practice of observing a location or person systematically, building a mental map of entrances, exits, and patterns of activity, which requires patience and deliberate attention. Operatives avoid staring and instead use quick glances that capture details without drawing attention, blending into the environment while maintaining awareness. Before observing, the operative must establish a baseline — the normal pattern of activity in the environment — because deviations from that baseline indicate something worth investigating: a person arriving at a cafe at the same time daily is baseline, while a stranger lingering near the entrance is a deviation. Mirroring is a technique in which the operative subtly matches the body language of the person being observed, building rapport and making the subject more comfortable. The operative should mirror posture, gesture frequency, and speech pace, but must avoid being obvious, since conscious mirroring can create suspicion.
