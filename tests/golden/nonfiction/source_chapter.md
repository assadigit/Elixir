# Source Chapter: The Art of Observation (nonfiction, fictionalized)

Effective observation is the cornerstone of intelligence work. The
ability to notice details that others miss distinguishes the
professional operative from the amateur. This chapter explores the
key techniques that enable observers to gather information without
arousing suspicion.

## The Casing Technique

Casing is the practice of observing a location or person
systematically. The operative develops a mental map of the
environment, noting entrances, exits, and patterns of activity.
This technique requires patience and deliberate attention.

The operative should avoid staring. Instead, they use quick glances
that capture details without drawing attention. The goal is to
blend into the environment while maintaining awareness.

## Establishing a Baseline

Before observing, the operative must establish a baseline. The
baseline is the normal pattern of activity in the environment.
Deviations from the baseline indicate something worth investigating.

For example, a person who arrives at a cafe every day at the same
time is part of the baseline. A stranger who lingers near the
entrance is a deviation.

## Mirroring Behavior

Mirroring is a technique where the operative subtly matches the
body language of the person they are observing. This builds rapport
and makes the subject more comfortable.

The operative should mirror posture, gesture frequency, and speech
pace. However, they must avoid being obvious, as conscious mirroring
can create suspicion.
