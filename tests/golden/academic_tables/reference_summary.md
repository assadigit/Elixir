The study assembled records for 142 grid-scale battery installations commissioned between 2015 and 2023, covering 31,400 megawatt-hours and 87% of global deployments in that window. Lithium iron phosphate cells retained 87.2% of capacity after eight years, beating nickel manganese cobalt by 5.7 percentage points, while lithium titanate degraded slowest at a 2.4 times capital cost premium. A discounted cash flow model with a 7% real discount rate and 20-year life shows levelized costs of storage falling 38% between 2018 and 2023, driven mainly by cell price declines, with the interquartile spread narrowing 21%. Assets stacking three or more revenue streams reached payback in 6.2 years versus 9.8 years for single-stream merchant assets, and frequency regulation alone never recouped capital costs. Sensitivity analysis finds payback most elastic to cycle frequency, ahead of capital cost and degradation assumptions. The authors caution that the sample over-represents utility-scale assets and that contracted hedges were unobservable, so reported costs are an upper bound for distributed storage.
