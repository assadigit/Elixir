## Key Distinctions

- Paranoid personality disorder vs. paranoid schizophrenia: The chapter distinguishes the two by noting that while both involve paranoid themes, paranoid schizophrenia involves a serious break with reality, bizarre delusions, auditory hallucinations, and thought control symptoms, whereas paranoid personality disorder is characterized by a stable, nondelusional state of mistrust without these severe psychotic features (p. 156).
- Paranoid personality disorder vs. paranoia or "delusional disorder: The text notes that while both conditions show few exacerbations/remissions and preserved functioning, the line is harder to draw but largely quantitative; paranoia involves actual delusions, whereas paranoid personality disorder does not, though its suspicions have a proverbial kernel of truth" and are more diffuse (p. 156).
- Paranoid personality vs. phobic character: The chapter explicitly distinguishes these by stating that phobic characters "avoid situations, not people," lack the personalization/intentionality of paranoid menace, and display anxiety/fear rather than rage/contempt, with avoidance as the primary defense rather than attack (p. 157).
- Paranoid personality vs. compulsive personality: Both share rigidity and limited spontaneity, but the chapter distinguishes them by noting the compulsive views moral pressure as internal and unquestionably superior, while the paranoid views it as external and unjustified; cognitively, the compulsive is overinclusive and indecisive, whereas the paranoid has narrowly focused, biased attention and is smugly self-assured (p. 158).
- Paranoid personality vs. narcissistic personality: Both share ambition, haughtiness, and splitting as a primary defense, but the chapter distinguishes them by noting that the paranoid displays pervasive mistrust, litigiousness, and lacks the charming social veneer and sexual promiscuity of the narcissist, while the narcissist hides discomfort with spontaneity via crafty socialization rather than angry negativism (p. 159).
