## Key Distinctions

- Paranoid personality disorder vs. paranoid schizophrenia: The chapter distinguishes the former as a nondelusional, stable state lacking a clear onset from the latter, which involves a serious break with reality, bizarre delusions, and hallucinations. (p. ~137)
- Paranoid personality disorder vs. phobic character: While both display restricted life-styles, the phobic character avoids situations without personalizing intention, reacts with anxiety rather than rage, and represents a higher-level (neurotic) organization, whereas the paranoid individual attacks or provokes based on perceived intent and displays a lower-level (borderline) organization. (p. ~141)
- Paranoid personality disorder vs. compulsive personality disorder: Both share rigidity and limited affect, but the compulsive recognizes internal sources of pressure and displays overinclusive cognition, while the paranoid attributes demands to external unjustified sources and exhibits narrowly focused, biased attention. (p. ~146)
- Paranoid personality disorder vs. narcissistic personality disorder: While both show splitting and ambition, the narcissist displays a charming social veneer and inordinate exploitativeness with absentmindedness, whereas the paranoid is characterized by pervasive mistrust, litigiousness, angry negativism, and acutely vigilant attention. (p. ~150)
- Paranoid personality disorder vs. antisocial personality disorder: The overlap exists, but the paranoid character lacks the chronicity of antisocial behavior and the child-to-adult continuum; their acts are consistent with projection rather than the stimulating quality seen in antisocial personalities. (p. ~155)
